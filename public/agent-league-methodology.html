Overview

The Agent League OVR (Overall Rating) system evaluates agent performance on a 0-100 scale using seven weighted attributes. Inspired by sports analytics (EA Sports Madden, FIFA), each agent receives a composite rating that reflects their contribution across production, speed, efficiency, and engagement — in minutes (the currency of the business).

Purpose: A single, transparent number that captures total agent value. Not a disciplinary tool — a development and recognition system. High OVR = high impact. Low OVR = coaching opportunity.
What changed in v1.2:
• Pickup Rate and Decline Rate removed — TaskRouter reservation data penalized agents for having teammates (when a call rings for 5 agents, only 1 can answer; the other 4 look like they "missed" it).
• Weights redistributed to 7 attributes. Talk Time boosted to 18% — minutes on calls = billable dollars.
• Bayesian confidence adjustment added — agents with few calls are pulled toward team average. Chris with 3 calls no longer gets crushed just because sample size is small.
• Opportunity-weighted normalization — agents working off-peak hours are rated against calls-available during their shift, not raw volume.
• Jose (CEO) override — gets a fixed 100 across the board with a 👑 marker, sorted to the bottom of leaderboards so he doesn't compete with working agents.

Weight rationale: Revenue generation (Conversions + Conv% + Talk Time) = 54% of the rating. Volume + Speed + Conv/Min = 36% for throughput and responsiveness. Wrap-Up = 10% for efficiency. Minutes and conversions dominate because they directly drive money — for both the client (appointments) and for JC (billable time).
Bayesian Confidence Adjustment

The OVR blends raw performance with the team average based on sample size:

OVR = (teamAvg × 10 + rawOvr × callsToday) / (10 + callsToday)

At 3 calls, your OVR is 77% team average + 23% your performance. At 40 calls, it's 20% team average + 80% yours. This prevents agents with tiny sample sizes from being crushed by noise — and prevents hot streaks from being overweighted early.

Example (Chris, 3 calls today):
Raw OVR (from 3 calls): 44 (below average because of volume curves)
Team avg OVR: 65
Bayesian OVR: (65×10 + 44×3) / 13 = 60 — fair, acknowledges small sample
Without Bayesian he'd be at 44 (DEV tier) which is unfair for doing nothing wrong.

Opportunity-Weighted Normalization

Chris works 3pm–midnight. Burke works 7am–6pm. If 80% of daily calls land in Burke's window and only 15% in Chris's, rating them the same way is unfair — Chris literally has fewer opportunities to handle.

opportunityWeight = calls-during-shift / total-daily-calls effectiveMinutes = scheduledMinutes × opportunityWeight Burke (peak): 480 min × 0.80 = 384 effective min Chris (off-peak): 540 min × 0.15 = 81 effective min Volume rating: calls / effectiveMinutes Conv/Min: conversions / effectiveMinutes Talk Time: talkMin / effectiveMinutes

Chris handling 15 calls on 81 effective minutes is rated equivalently to Burke handling 40 calls on 384 effective minutes. Both captured ~9 calls per effective minute of opportunity.

Normalization Curves (0-97 Effective Scale)

Each raw metric is converted to a 0-97 sub-rating. 99 is unreachable by formula — reserved as a theoretical ceiling. The curves use diminishing returns: going from 85 to 90 is much harder than going from 50 to 55. Volume, Conv/Min, and Talk Time are normalized by opportunity-adjusted minutes — your shift minutes times your call density weight.

The 👑 CEO Override

Jose is the CEO. He occasionally jumps on the phones, but his role is not to compete with working agents on call volume. To handle this cleanly:

  • Fixed OVR of 100 with all 7 sub-ratings at 100.
  • Sorted to the bottom of the main leaderboard regardless of sort direction — he doesn't compete for rank #1.
  • Crown (👑) marker instead of a rank number.
  • Excluded from top 3 category competitions (Conversions, Calls, Speed, Conv%, Most Minutes, vs Career).
  • Excluded from team average calculation — he doesn't inflate the Bayesian prior.
  • His real metrics still appear in his row. The 100s are the rating; his actual calls/speed/talk time are shown accurately in the raw-value columns.

Known Methodology Weaknesses

Transparent about where the system still falls short:

  • No call quality metric. The system measures speed, volume, and outcomes — but not how well an agent handles a difficult call. A future "Quality Score" from call reviews or client satisfaction would add an 8th attribute.
  • Conversion attribution is coarse. An agent who warms a lead across 3 calls gets no credit if a different agent logs the final conversion. Multi-touch attribution would be more fair.
  • Historical baselines are thin. The accumulator currently only has partial March 2026 data. Trend arrows will become meaningful as months accumulate.
  • Weekend/off-peak hours use opportunity weighting but don't account for the psychological weight of being alone on a shift. A Sunday overnight agent handling one weird call at 3am isn't the same as a Tuesday agent taking call #20.
  • Blended agents (Wendy, Sara) appear in both JC and MSC views. Their OVR reflects total volume across both brands. Brand-level splitting would be more precise.
  • Outbound agents (William, Joseph) are not currently rated — the system is designed for inbound call handling, not outbound sales. They appear on the Outbound page with their own metrics.
  • Pickup Rate is intentionally excluded. TaskRouter reservation data is misleading — a 35% rate doesn't mean 65% missed, it means teammates answered first. Including it penalizes agents for having a team.
